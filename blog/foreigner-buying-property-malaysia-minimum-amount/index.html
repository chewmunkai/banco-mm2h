Foreigner buying property in Malaysia: the minimum amount, state by state (2026)

Every Malaysian state sets its own minimum price for foreign buyers, and for an MM2H applicant the higher of that floor and the tier minimum applies. Here are the 2026 floors, how sure we are of each one, and the price you will actually pay in each state and tier.

The minimum amount for a foreigner buying property in Malaysia isn't one number. Land is a state matter, so each state sets its own floor: RM 1 million in Kuala Lumpur, RM 2 million in most of Selangor, up to RM 3 million for a landed house on Penang Island. If you are moving on MM2H, the tier's own minimum (RM 600,000 for Silver) sits on top of that, and the higher of the two applies. This guide gives the 2026 floors, shows how much each one can be trusted, and works out the price you will really pay in each state and tier.

Key takeaways

  • Yes, foreigners can buy property in Malaysia, freehold or leasehold, but every purchase needs the state's consent and must clear the state's minimum price.
  • The floors (reported, 2026): Kuala Lumpur RM 1M · Selangor RM 2M (zones 1–2) or RM 1M (zone 3) · Penang Island RM 1M for condos, RM 3M for landed · Johor RM 1M · Sabah RM 600k for condos, RM 1M for landed.
  • For MM2H Silver, RM 600,000 is rarely the real price. In KL, Selangor, Penang Island and Johor, Silver applicants must spend at least RM 1 million, and in Petaling Jaya RM 2 million.
  • On top of the price: foreign buyers' stamp duty, reported at a flat 8% from 1 January 2026, plus a 3% state levy in Penang and Johor.
  • No state publishes a current official table online. Confirm the floor for your exact property with a conveyancing lawyer before you pay a deposit.

Can foreigners buy property in Malaysia?

Yes. Non-citizens can own residential property in Malaysia outright, with their name on the title. Two conditions apply everywhere:

  1. State consent. Under section 433B of the National Land Code, a non-citizen needs the state authority's approval to acquire land, and that includes a condo unit. Your lawyer applies for it after you sign. It adds weeks to the purchase.
  2. A minimum price. The federal guideline raised the floor for foreign buyers to RM 1 million per unit in 2014. Because land is a state matter, each state then set its own, and they now differ widely.

Some property is off-limits to foreigners in every state: low-cost and low-to-medium-cost housing, homes on Malay reserve land, and units set aside for Bumiputera buyers in a development.

What the floors mean for MM2H Silver, Gold and Platinum

MOTAC's terms require every MM2H participant to buy a home after approval, at a minimum of RM 600,000 for Silver, RM 1 million for Gold and RM 2 million for Platinum, and keep it for 10 years. (See MM2H Silver vs Gold vs Platinum for the rest of each tier.) The state floor still applies to you as a foreign buyer, so the price you really pay is whichever is higher.

In every state where the floor is well reported, Silver's RM 600,000 isn't the real minimum. If you want to live in Kuala Lumpur, Selangor, Penang Island or Johor, budget at least RM 1 million for the home, and RM 2 million around Petaling Jaya.

Stamp duty and state levies on top of the price

  • Stamp duty. Budget 2026 raised the stamp duty foreign buyers pay on the transfer from 4% to a flat 8%, for transfers from 1 January 2026, according to KPMG's Budget 2026 alert and The Star. Some outlets describe it as a 4–8% range, so ask your lawyer to confirm the rate for your transfer. Permanent residents are excluded; MM2H holders are not.
  • Penang: a 3% levy on the purchase price for foreign buyers (reported).
  • Johor: a levy of 3%, with a minimum of RM 30,000, for sale agreements signed from 1 July 2025, up from 2% (reported).
  • Everywhere: legal fees, the state consent fee and the loan costs, if you borrow.

How to buy property in Malaysia as a foreigner, step by step

  1. Confirm the floor for the exact state, district and property type, plus any levy, with a conveyancing lawyer in that state.
  2. If you're on MM2H, get approved first. MOTAC's terms require the home to be bought after approval. Under the SEZ/SFZ category in Johor, it must be bought directly from a developer.
  3. Sign the sale agreement with state consent as a condition, so the deal falls away if consent is refused.
  4. Your lawyer applies for state consent. Expect it to add weeks.
  5. Pay stamp duty and complete the transfer. On MM2H, the home can't be sold for 10 years.

How we'd use this

Banco's view Our judgement, not an official rule.

  • Choose the place before the tier. In Kuala Lumpur, Penang Island or Johor, Silver and Gold need the same RM 1M home. The only real difference between them there is the deposit (USD 150,000 vs USD 500,000) and the pass length (5 vs 15 years).
  • Around Petaling Jaya, every tier pays RM 2M. If you want Selangor but not a RM 2M home, look at Kuala Lumpur itself (RM 1M) before you look at a higher tier.
  • Where Silver might still mean RM 600,000: Sabah condos (reported) and possibly some states where the figures conflict. Confirm before you plan around it.
  • The SEZ/SFZ category in Johor is a separate route with its own rules, covered in our tier guide. The developer-only rule is the part people miss.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum amount for a foreigner buying property in Malaysia?

It depends on the state. Reported 2026 floors: RM 1 million in Kuala Lumpur and Johor; RM 2 million in most of Selangor (RM 1 million in Hulu Selangor and Sabak Bernam); RM 1 million for a condo and RM 3 million for a landed home on Penang Island; RM 600,000 for a condo and RM 1 million for a landed home in Sabah. Confirm with a lawyer in that state.

Can foreigners buy property in Malaysia without MM2H?

Yes. You don't need MM2H to buy, and MM2H doesn't exempt you from state consent or the state floor. MM2H adds its own tier minimum, and the higher of the two applies.

Can foreigners buy property in Kuala Lumpur?

Yes, from a reported minimum of RM 1 million for condos and landed homes, with state consent.

Can foreigners buy property in Selangor?

Yes, but the floor is RM 2 million in zones 1 and 2, which include Petaling Jaya, Subang, Shah Alam, Klang and Cyberjaya's district (Sepang), and RM 1 million in Hulu Selangor and Sabak Bernam.

Can foreigners buy freehold property in Malaysia?

Yes. Foreigners can buy freehold or leasehold homes. The same state consent and minimum price apply to both.

Can foreigners buy land in Malaysia?

Generally not agricultural land or Malay reserve land. Other land needs state consent under section 433B of the National Land Code, and states can refuse it. Ask a conveyancing lawyer before you look at land.

Can foreigners buy property in Forest City?

Yes. Forest City is in Johor's Special Financial Zone, where some exceptions to the usual floor have been reported for new units. If you're using the MM2H SEZ/SFZ category, the home must be bought directly from a developer.

How much stamp duty does a foreigner pay on Malaysian property?

Reported at a flat 8% of the price or market value from 1 January 2026, up from 4%. Some sources describe a 4–8% range, so confirm with your lawyer. Penang and Johor also charge a state levy of about 3%.
