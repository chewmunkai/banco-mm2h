Moving to Malaysia from the USA starts with a long-stay pass. For most Americans that's MM2H (Silver: a USD 150,000 fixed deposit, a home in Malaysia and a 5-year pass), or DE Rantau for remote workers. Then come the American rules that follow you abroad. The IRS taxes US citizens on worldwide income wherever they live. There's no US–Malaysia income tax treaty. Your MM2H deposit alone means filing an FBAR every year. And Medicare generally doesn't cover care outside the US. This guide covers each one, with the official source.

Key takeaways

  • Visas: MM2H from age 25 (Silver USD 150,000 deposit; Gold USD 500,000; Platinum USD 1M), the Johor SEZ/SFZ category from age 21 (USD 32,000–65,000), or DE Rantau for remote workers.
  • US tax: you still file a US return and owe US tax on worldwide income. Malaysia isn't on the IRS list of treaty countries.
  • FBAR: foreign accounts totalling more than USD 10,000 at any point in the year must be reported. A USD 150,000 MM2H deposit is well over that.
  • Malaysian tax: foreign-sourced income received by resident individuals is exempt in Malaysia until 31 December 2036.
  • Benefits: check Social Security with SSA's Payments Abroad Screening Tool. Medicare generally won't pay for care in Malaysia, so you'll need private insurance there.

Social Security in Malaysia

The SSA decides country by country whether it can send payments abroad. We couldn't confirm Malaysia's status on an official page we were able to access, so use SSA's Payments Abroad Screening Tool before you move. Tell SSA your new address, and keep a US bank account if you'd rather not depend on international transfers.

Medicare and health insurance

Medicare.gov says Medicare generally doesn't cover health care while you're outside the US, with narrow exceptions that don't apply to living in Malaysia. In practice:

  • You'll need private health insurance in Malaysia. MM2H also requires health insurance when you renew the pass.
  • Think before you drop Medicare Part B. If you cancel it and re-enrol later, you may pay a late-enrolment penalty. Ask Medicare before you cancel.
  • Some Medigap plans include limited foreign-travel emergency cover. It's not a substitute for living-abroad insurance.

Frequently asked questions

Can Americans move to Malaysia?

Yes. Most use MM2H, which from age 25 asks for a fixed deposit (USD 150,000 for Silver) and a home in Malaysia, with no offshore-income test. Remote workers can use DE Rantau.

What are the Malaysia retirement visa requirements for US citizens?

The same as for any nationality. For MM2H Silver: age 25+, a USD 150,000 fixed deposit, a home bought after approval, 90 days a year in Malaysia, a medical check-up, and an application through a MOTAC-licensed agent.

Is there a tax treaty between the US and Malaysia?

No. Malaysia isn't on the IRS's list of US income tax treaty countries (updated 3 January 2026).

Do I need to file an FBAR for my MM2H deposit?

Yes, if your foreign accounts total more than USD 10,000 at any point in the year. A USD 150,000 MM2H deposit is over that on its own.

Can I get Social Security in Malaysia?

Check with SSA's Payments Abroad Screening Tool before you move. We couldn't confirm Malaysia's status on an official page we could access.

Does Medicare cover me in Malaysia?

Generally no. Medicare doesn't cover care outside the US except in narrow cases. Buy private health insurance for Malaysia.
