Moving to Malaysia from the UK means choosing a long-stay pass (for most Britons, MM2H, or DE Rantau if you work remotely), and then dealing with three British questions. Your State Pension will be frozen at the rate you first get abroad, because Malaysia isn't on the UK's list of countries that get annual increases. Your pensions will be taxed according to the UK–Malaysia tax treaty and Malaysia's foreign-income exemption, which runs to 2036. And once you live abroad, you lose free NHS care. This guide covers each one, with the official source.

Key takeaways

  • Visas: MM2H (from age 25; Silver needs a USD 150,000 fixed deposit, 5-year pass), the Johor SEZ/SFZ category (from age 21; USD 32,000–65,000) or DE Rantau for remote workers.
  • State Pension: you can claim it in Malaysia, but it won't rise each year. Malaysia isn't on DWP's list of countries that get the annual increase.
  • Tax: the UK and Malaysia have a double taxation agreement. Malaysia exempts foreign-sourced income received by resident individuals until 31 December 2036.
  • NHS: when you move abroad permanently you're generally no longer entitled to free NHS care. Budget for private health insurance in Malaysia.
  • Not permanent residency. MM2H is a long-term visit pass and doesn't lead to PR.

Your UK State Pension in Malaysia: frozen

You can claim the UK State Pension while living in Malaysia. But it only goes up each year if you live in a country on the DWP's list: the EEA, Gibraltar, Switzerland and countries with a qualifying social security agreement. Malaysia isn't on that list. Your pension stays at the rate you first receive abroad.

If you come back to live in a country on the list, the pension goes up to the current rate from then on, but the missed increases aren't back-paid.

Tax when you move from the UK to Malaysia

Three rules interact. This section explains them; it isn't tax advice.

  1. Leaving UK tax residence. The UK's statutory residence test decides whether you're still UK-resident for tax. Leaving the UK doesn't automatically end it, and UK-source income such as rent can stay taxable in the UK.
  2. The UK–Malaysia double taxation agreement. It decides which country may tax each kind of income, including different kinds of pension, so you aren't taxed twice on the same money. HMRC publishes the full text.
  3. Malaysia's foreign-income exemption. Foreign-sourced income received in Malaysia by resident individuals is exempt until 31 December 2036, under an exemption order gazetted as P.U.(A) 451/2024. You're generally tax-resident in Malaysia if you spend 182 days or more there in a calendar year. MM2H itself only asks for 90.

Take this to a UK–Malaysia cross-border tax adviser before you move: each pension you hold (State, workplace, private, government service), any UK rental income, and how many days you expect to spend in each country. Depending on the type, a pension may be taxable only in the UK, only in Malaysia, or both with credit for tax paid.

Healthcare: replacing the NHS

The NHS says that if you move abroad permanently, you're generally no longer entitled to free NHS treatment as you were when you lived in the UK. In Malaysia you'll use private hospitals and clinics, paid for by you or your insurer.

  • MM2H requires health insurance when you renew the pass, along with a medical report, and applicants have a medical check-up at a MOTAC panel clinic.
  • Insure before you need it. International health policies get dearer and more restrictive with age and pre-existing conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Can I move to Malaysia from the UK?

Yes. Most British retirees and families use MM2H, which from age 25 asks for a fixed deposit (USD 150,000 for Silver) and a home in Malaysia. Remote workers can use DE Rantau. You apply for MM2H through a MOTAC-licensed agent.

What are the requirements to retire to Malaysia from the UK?

For MM2H Silver: age 25+, a USD 150,000 fixed deposit in a Malaysian bank, a home bought after approval (at least RM 600,000, or the state floor if higher), 90 days a year in Malaysia, and a medical check-up. There's no offshore-income test.

Will my UK State Pension go up if I live in Malaysia?

No. Malaysia isn't on DWP's list of countries where the State Pension is increased each year, so it stays at the rate you first receive abroad.

Is there a tax treaty between the UK and Malaysia?

Yes. The UK–Malaysia double taxation agreement decides which country taxes each type of income. HMRC publishes it on GOV.UK.

Does Malaysia tax my UK pension?

Malaysia currently exempts foreign-sourced income received by resident individuals, until 31 December 2036. Whether the UK taxes a given pension depends on its type and the treaty. Ask a cross-border tax adviser.

Can I still use the NHS after moving to Malaysia?

Generally not for free once you live abroad permanently. Plan on private health insurance in Malaysia, which MM2H also requires at renewal.
