MM2H Johor gives you two routes. The first is regular MM2H (Silver, Gold or Platinum), the same as anywhere in Peninsular Malaysia. The second is the SEZ/SFZ category, which covers Johor's special zones, including Forest City. It starts at a USD 32,000 fixed deposit for applicants aged 50 or over, from age 21, with a 10-year pass. The catch is in MOTAC's terms: the home must be bought directly from a developer, and you can't sell it for 10 years. This guide puts the two side by side, with the Johor levy and the Singapore rail link that's driving demand.

Key takeaways

  • SEZ/SFZ: fixed deposit USD 65,000 (age 21–49) or USD 32,000 (age 50+), 10-year pass, RM 300 to renew every 5 years. No work or business.
  • The trap: the SEZ/SFZ home must be bought direct from a developer, never resale, and kept for 10 years. The price floor follows Johor's own policy.
  • Regular MM2H in Johor Bahru: Silver's USD 150,000 deposit, but the home must clear Johor's reported RM 1M floor, not Silver's RM 600,000.
  • Costs on top (reported): Johor's 3% levy (minimum RM 30,000) for sales signed from 1 July 2025, plus 8% stamp duty for foreign buyers from 1 January 2026.
  • The RTS Link from Bukit Chagar to Woodlands North in Singapore is expected to open to passengers in February 2027.

The SEZ/SFZ catch: developer-direct only

MOTAC's SEZ/SFZ terms say the home must be bought from a developer, not through an agent and not from an existing owner. In practice that means:

  • No resale homes. You choose from what developers in the zone are selling now.
  • No easy price check. With no resale market to compare against, you rely on the developer's price.
  • No exit for 10 years. You can upgrade to another property, but you can't sell and leave the scheme with your money.

Before you sign, get an independent valuation and ask the developer for its recent sale prices in the same block. The low deposit is real, but the home is where the money goes, and you're locked in for a decade.

Johor property costs for foreign buyers

Outside the special zones, Johor's floor for foreign buyers is reported at RM 1 million for condos and landed homes, with RM 2M reported for landed homes in some international zones. Inside Forest City and other special zones, lower floors or none have been reported for some new units, but only in developer and portal sources. We haven't confirmed them.

The RTS Link: why Johor Bahru is moving up

The Johor Bahru–Singapore Rapid Transit System Link runs about 4 km from Bukit Chagar in Johor Bahru to Woodlands North in Singapore, a journey of about five minutes. The New Straits Times reported in October 2026 that the passenger service is expected to start in February 2027.

Banco's view If you'll visit Singapore often, a home near Bukit Chagar or central Johor Bahru is a different proposition from Forest City, which is further west. Remember that neither MM2H nor SEZ/SFZ lets you work in Malaysia, and working in Singapore needs a Singapore work pass. Check both sides before you plan a commute.

Which Johor route fits you

Banco's view Our judgement, not an official rule.

  • SEZ/SFZ fits if you're 50 or over, happy with a new-build in the zone, and you value a 10-year pass on a small deposit more than the freedom to choose any home.
  • Regular Silver fits if you want a resale home, a home in central Johor Bahru, or the option to live anywhere in Peninsular Malaysia later. You'll pay at least RM 1M for the home either way.
  • For applicants under 25, SEZ/SFZ is the only MM2H route, since the regular tiers start at 25.

Frequently asked questions

What are the MM2H Johor requirements?

Regular MM2H in Johor has the same requirements as elsewhere: from age 25, a USD 150,000 fixed deposit for Silver and a home in Malaysia, which in Johor must clear a reported RM 1M floor. The SEZ/SFZ category is open from age 21, with a USD 65,000 deposit (USD 32,000 from age 50) and a home bought directly from a developer.

What are the MM2H Forest City requirements?

Forest City is a Special Financial Zone, so the SEZ/SFZ category applies: age 21+, a USD 65,000 or USD 32,000 fixed deposit, a home bought directly from a developer and kept for 10 years, and 90 days a year in Malaysia. The pass lasts 10 years.

Can foreigners buy property in Forest City, Malaysia?

Yes. Some exceptions to Johor's usual RM 1M floor have been reported for new units in the zone, but only in developer and portal sources. Under the MM2H SEZ/SFZ category, you must buy directly from the developer.

Can I sell my SEZ/SFZ home?

Not for 10 years. MOTAC's terms allow you to upgrade to another property in that time.

Can I work in Singapore on MM2H Johor?

MM2H and SEZ/SFZ passes don't allow work in Malaysia. Working in Singapore is a matter for Singapore's own work-pass rules. Check both before you plan to commute.

When does the Johor–Singapore RTS Link open?

The New Straits Times reported in October 2026 that the passenger service is expected to start in February 2027, running from Bukit Chagar to Woodlands North in about five minutes.
