MM2H Silver vs Gold vs Platinum comes down to four numbers: the fixed deposit you place in a Malaysian bank (USD 150,000, USD 500,000 or USD 1,000,000), the home you must buy (from RM 600,000, RM 1 million or RM 2 million), the length of the pass (5, 15 or 20 years) and whether you may work (Platinum only). This guide sets the tiers side by side using Malaysia's official terms, shows where those terms disagree with each other, and ends with how we would choose.

Key takeaways

  • Silver: USD 150,000 fixed deposit, home from RM 600,000, 5-year pass, renewable. The entry tier.
  • Gold: USD 500,000, home from RM 1 million, 15-year pass. Same rights as Silver, three times as long between renewals.
  • Platinum: USD 1,000,000, home from RM 2 million, 20-year pass, and the only tier that lets you work or run a business. Its participating fee is RM 200,000.
  • All three: minimum age 25, 90 days a year in Malaysia, no offshore-income test, and you must apply through a MOTAC-licensed agent.
  • MM2H is a long-term visit pass. It is not permanent residency.

How much each tier asks you to deposit

The fixed deposit is the biggest number in every tier. It must sit in a Malaysian bank licensed under the Financial Services Act 2013 or the Islamic Financial Services Act 2013. It stays your money, but you can't spend it freely.

The 50% rule

Every tier lets you withdraw up to half of the deposit's principal, but only for four purposes in Malaysia. The other half stays put for as long as you hold the pass.

How long each pass lasts

The pass comes with a multiple-entry visa for you and your dependents. When the first term ends, every tier renews in 5-year blocks. You'll need a valid passport, your latest medical report and health insurance.

The pass sticker in your passport can't outlast the passport itself. If your passport expires first, you renew the sticker into the new one.

MM2H Silver requirements: who it suits

Silver is the entry tier: USD 150,000 on deposit, a home of at least RM 600,000, and a 5-year pass. You can't work or run a business on it. If you need to, the terms say you must apply for the relevant pass separately.

Is MM2H Silver renewable? Yes. After the first 5 years it renews every 5 years, with a renewal fee of RM 1,500.

The RM 600,000 floor is the MM2H minimum, not always the real one. Each Malaysian state sets its own minimum price for foreign buyers, and in many urban areas it is higher than RM 600,000. The higher figure applies. Check the floor for the exact state and property type before you budget for Silver.

MM2H Gold requirements, and Gold vs Silver

Gold asks for USD 500,000 on deposit and a home of at least RM 1 million, and gives you a 15-year pass. Its rights are the same as Silver's: no work or business, the same dependents, the same 90-day stay rule.

So MM2H Gold vs Silver is really one question: is 15 years without renewing worth an extra USD 350,000 parked in a Malaysian bank? For someone planning to stay a long time, with funds that would otherwise sit in a deposit anyway, it can be. For most first-time applicants it isn't, because Silver renews.

MM2H Platinum requirements, and Gold vs Platinum

Platinum asks for USD 1,000,000 on deposit, a home of at least RM 2 million and a one-off participating fee of RM 200,000. In return you get a 20-year pass and two rights no other tier has:

  • Business, investment and career activities are permitted. Silver, Gold and SEZ/SFZ holders must apply for a separate pass.
  • A foreign maid can be registered as a dependent.

MM2H Gold vs Platinum is therefore mostly about work. If you'll earn or run a business in Malaysia, Platinum is the only tier that allows it on the same pass. If you won't, Gold gives you 15 years of the same residence rights for half the deposit and a fraction of the fee.

Rules that apply to every tier

  • No offshore-income requirement. MOTAC dropped it for every category.
  • Minimum stay: 90 days a year in Malaysia, cumulative. If you are 25–49, your dependents' days can count.
  • Dependents: your spouse; children under 21; children aged 21–34 if single and unemployed; children with a medically certified disability, at any age; your parents and parents-in-law.
  • Education: children may study up to university level at government-recognised institutions, on the MM2H pass or with a Student Pass issued automatically.
  • Medical: a check-up at a MOTAC panel clinic or hospital after approval; long-term medical treatment in Malaysia is allowed.
  • Your home: you must buy and own one after approval, and you can't sell it for 10 years. You can upgrade to a more expensive home.
  • If the principal dies, the pass can transfer to the next of kin among the registered dependents.
  • Tax: MOTAC lists a "tax exemption on incoming funds such as FD". Read it narrowly. It isn't a general tax ruling, and it doesn't change what you owe at home. US citizens, for example, are taxed by the IRS on worldwide income wherever they live.
  • Breaking any condition can get the pass revoked.

The fourth option: SEZ/SFZ in Johor

MOTAC also runs a cheaper category tied to Johor's Special Economic Zone and Special Financial Zone. It is open from age 21 and asks for a fixed deposit of USD 65,000 (age 21–49) or USD 32,000 (age 50+). The pass lasts 10 years, the participating fee is RM 1,000 and renewal is RM 300.

The catch: the home must be bought directly from a developer, not through an agent or from an existing owner, and the price floor follows Johor's state rules. Work and business aren't allowed. It suits buyers who already want to live in Johor. It's a poor fit if you want Kuala Lumpur or Penang.

Four things MM2H is not

  1. It isn't permanent residency. It's a long-term visit pass with a multiple-entry visa. Nothing in the current terms leads to PR or citizenship.
  2. It isn't priced in ringgit. The deposits are set in US dollars. Pages that quote "RM 500,000 for Silver" are converting, often wrongly.
  3. It isn't free access to your deposit. You can withdraw at most half, and only for the four purposes above.
  4. It isn't a do-it-yourself application. You can't apply directly. See the next section.

How to apply, and who actually lodges it

MOTAC's terms are explicit: an application "should be submitted and completed through an MM2H tour operating business that has been licensed by the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture". Every application goes through the One Stop Centre MM2H, and the final approval belongs to the Immigration Department under the Ministry of Home Affairs.

Frequently asked questions

Is MM2H Silver renewable?

Yes. The Silver pass runs for 5 years and can then be renewed every 5 years. The renewal fee is RM 1,500, and you'll need a valid passport, your latest medical report and health insurance.

What is the difference between MM2H Gold and Silver?

Gold needs a USD 500,000 fixed deposit and a home of at least RM 1 million, and gives a 15-year pass. Silver needs USD 150,000 and a home of at least RM 600,000, and gives a 5-year pass. Neither allows work or business, and both allow the same dependents.

What are the MM2H Platinum requirements?

A USD 1,000,000 fixed deposit in a licensed Malaysian bank, a minimum age of 25, a one-off participating fee of RM 200,000, and the purchase of a home worth at least RM 2 million after approval. The pass lasts 20 years and is renewable.

Can I work in Malaysia on MM2H?

Only on Platinum, which permits business, investment and career activities. Silver, Gold and SEZ/SFZ holders must apply for the relevant pass separately.

What is the minimum age for MM2H?

25 for Silver, Gold and Platinum. The SEZ/SFZ category in Johor is open from age 21.

Does MM2H give permanent residency?

No. MM2H is a long-term visit pass with a multiple-entry visa. The current terms contain no route to permanent residency or citizenship.

Can I withdraw my MM2H fixed deposit?

Up to 50% of the principal, only to buy a home, or for education, medical treatment or tourism in Malaysia. MOTAC's terms say this is allowed after approval; the One Stop Centre's 2025 sheet says after one year. Confirm the timing with your licensed agent.

Do I need to show offshore income for MM2H in 2026?

No. MOTAC dropped the offshore-income requirement for every category.

Can I apply for MM2H myself, without an agent?

No. Applications must go through an MM2H agent licensed by MOTAC. Banco's applications are lodged by PAKS (MM2H) Sdn. Bhd., licence MM2H881.

Can my parents come with me on MM2H?

Yes. Parents and parents-in-law can be registered as dependents in every tier.
